# R222 lineage: the dimensional-model umbrella, retired

A lineage record, not a roadmap item; it lives in `audits/` so the roadmap-tool ignores it,
alongside the other permanent lineage documents. It preserves what remained load-bearing in
`roadmap/dimensional-model-pivot.md` (R222, "Dimensional model pivot: slots over cross-product
permits", filed 2026-05-21, discarded 2026-08-06) when the item was retired, and records where
each residue went. The disposition analysis is in
`2026-08-06-fact-base-impact-sweep.md`; the earlier conformance record is
`2026-07-04-r222-r333-conformance-analysis.md`.

## Why retired

R222's thesis won and its mechanisms lost, one layer below where it was looking. The
slots-over-cross-product claim ("adding an axis becomes adding a slot; no multiplication") is
the fact-base's own justification: axes are per-directive `intent_` relations and the claim
view's arm list (R589). But two of its three load-bearing architectural claims were inverted by
that same architecture: producers do not read graphql-java primitives (capture decodes the AST
once; no graphql-java node crosses into the relations, so the `Walker<S, C>` substrate
vocabulary has no home), and validity does not ride a wrapper (`WalkerResult.Ok/Err` per
producer is replaced by violation rows; planning never re-checks, a failed coordinate simply
has no row to join). Its operation-axis content was delivered by R563 as the `OperationMember`
member multiset, its input-side `TableInputType` premise was deleted by R519, and its
stage-sequenced cutting plan was replaced by R589's strangler frame (migrate by consumer, not
by derivation layer). What was left was ~80 percent pointers; the residues below were cut out
and the file discarded.

## Previous design attempts (preserved verbatim in substance)

Rejected alternatives from the R222 drafts, preserved because several are arguments the
fact-base work re-runs at another layer.

- **Horizontal Phase 1 (all carriers, all slots, all `Diagnostic` arms up front).** Rejected:
  vocabulary that doesn't ride a live producer is a contract committed before any consumer
  pulls on it. Vertical slices each ship their own vocabulary. (The strangler frame's
  "a derivation is built when the first consumer needing it migrates" is this same argument.)
- **R164 + R226 as separate items.** Rejected because the work overlapped structurally;
  unified as one umbrella with explicit stage dependencies.
- **Recursive `InputUsage` carrier scoped to SQL emission.** One classified output that
  consumers re-discriminated by role. Rejected as wrong granularity.
- **`Invalid<Family>` arms inside the carrier families.** Rejected because no downstream
  generator inspects an `Invalid` arm; generation is blocked before any generator runs, so the
  asymmetry encoded an invariant the wrapper + phase split already enforced. (R589 re-runs
  this: violations are rows beside the claims, never arms inside the carriers.)
- **`Input` / `InputFieldDecl` per-input wrapper records.** Rejected because per-input identity
  had no carrier-independent state worth a record.
- **`SchemaCoordinate(String)` identity wrapper.** Stringly-typed, conflating type and field.
  Rejected; R333's decomposed-grammar-columns key is the correction carried to its end.
- **Optional carrier slots (`Optional<Pagination>`, ...).** Rejected for field-universal slots
  (the `No<Family>` arm makes absence first-class) and for directive-gated slots
  (interface-gated presence). Note the boundary the store draws: this rejection governed the
  *Java carrier* surface, while the capture relations encode absence as a NULL column or an
  absent row by design, with effective-value defaulting a derivation. The two conventions
  coexist because they answer different layers; the vocabulary clash is recorded in the impact
  sweep.
- **`ValidationShape` as a per-input carrier.** Rejected because validation fires at the
  resolver method-arg boundary, which is the output field's seat; the consumer is the unit of
  validation.
- **`MethodArguments` as the carrier name.** Replaced by `MethodCall` to align with the
  existing naming family.
