# Collapse the duplicated directive index and guard it

> The manual carries two hand-maintained directive indexes. `reference/directives/index.adoc`
> is the directives landing and is complete (29 entries). `reference/index.adoc` carries an
> inline *copy* of the same alphabetical and categorical lists, and that copy is stale: it is
> missing `@asFacet`, `@pivot`, `@routine`, `@scalarType`, and `@tenantFanOut`. Delete the
> duplicate, point the Reference chapter at the directives landing the way it already points at
> Runtime API and Diagnostics, and extend `DirectiveDocCoverageTest` so the surviving list is
> build-enforced against the page set instead of trusted.

---

## Motivation

A user went looking for `@pivot` in the directive reference and concluded the reference was out
of date. The page is not out of date: `docs/manual/reference/directives/pivot.adoc` is a
complete 111-line reference matching the declaration in `directives.graphqls`, and the generated
`docs/manual/_generated/supported-directives.adoc` fragment lists `@pivot` under Supported. What
was missing is the link from the page they landed on.

The manual landing routes its Reference quadrant to `reference/index.adoc`
(`docs/manual/index.adoc:26`). That page does not link to `reference/directives/index.adoc` at
all. Instead it reproduces the directives landing's two columns inline
(`reference/index.adoc:30-90`) and links straight from its own bullets to individual pages. So
the navigation path manual → Reference → Directives lands on the stale 24-entry list, with no
route from there to the complete one. The complete landing page is reachable only from
`quick-start.adoc:15`, `explanation/how-it-works.adoc`, `reference/runtime-api.adoc`,
`reference/diagnostics-glossary.adoc`, and `reference/mojo-configuration.adoc`: every chapter
except the one that owns it.

Five directives are invisible to anyone browsing the Reference chapter top-down. Two of them
(`@pivot`, `@asFacet`) are non-obvious features a reader is unlikely to find by guessing a URL.

## Current state

Measured against `graphitron/src/main/resources/no/sikt/graphitron/rewrite/schema/directives.graphqls`:

| Surface | Kind | Entries | Complete? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `directives.graphqls` | source of truth | 31 declared | n/a |
| `_generated/supported-directives.adoc` | generated by `DirectiveSupportReport` | 29 advertised | yes, derived |
| `reference/directives/*.adoc` | one page per directive | 29 pages | yes |
| `reference/directives/index.adoc` (alphabetical column) | hand-maintained | 29 xrefs | yes |
| `reference/directives/index.adoc` (*By category* column) | hand-maintained | 27 xrefs | **no, missing 2** |
| `reference/index.adoc` | hand-maintained duplicate | 24 xrefs | **no, missing 5** |

The surviving landing's *By category* column is not complete either: `@referenceFor` is filed under
no category at all, and `@record` lost the *Mapping* slot it holds in the stale copy
(`reference/index.adoc:64`) when `@scalarType` was added beside it. The alphabetical column is the
only surface in the table that is both hand-maintained and correct today.

(The 31/29 gap is `@experimental_constructType` and `@sourceRow`, both deliberately withheld from
the v1 surface and correctly page-less. Those are already tracked by
`roadmap/reintroduce-sourcerow-docs.md`.)

The divergence predates the visible git history. Both index files and `pivot.adoc` arrive in the
squashed root import and neither index has been edited since, so there is no commit that shows a
page being added without its index entry. The mechanism is structural rather than a single
missed step.

## Why no guard caught it

`DirectiveDocCoverageTest` (graphitron-sakila-example, unit tier) is the drift seam for directive
documentation and it works well for what it covers: a bidirectional check between the SDL
declarations and **file existence** under `docs/manual/reference/directives/`, with the
withheld-from-v1 carve-out derived from the generated fragment rather than duplicated. Adding
`pivot.adoc` satisfied it completely.

What it never does is open either index. Whether a page is *linked* from anywhere is outside its
contract, so the index lists are the one part of the directive documentation surface that is
purely trusted. The generated fragment does not help either: nothing cross-checks it against the
hand-written lists.

## Design decision: delete rather than sync

Three options were considered.

1. **Patch the five missing bullets into `reference/index.adoc`.** Rejected. It fixes today's
   symptom and leaves the mechanism: two lists, one of them still unguarded, drifting again on
   the next directive added.
2. **Delete the inline list from `reference/index.adoc`; replace the Directives section with a
   pointer to `reference/directives/index.adoc`.** Recommended. It matches the shape the same
   page already uses for its other sections: Runtime API, Maven plugin, Editor support,
   Diagnostics, and Deprecations are each a single xref plus a descriptive sentence, not an
   inlined roll-up of the target's contents. Directives is the only section that inlines, and it
   is the only one that has drifted.
3. **Generate the directive index from the SDL.** Rejected for now. The alphabetical column
   could be generated, but the deprecation and rejection annotations, and especially the *By
   category* grouping (Mapping, Joining, Querying, Mutating, Sorting, Error handling,
   Polymorphism, Global ID), are editorial judgement not derivable from `directives.graphqls`.
   Generating half the page and hand-maintaining the other half would trade one drift seam for a
   more confusing one. The guard in the next section gets the same protection at a fraction of
   the cost.

   This is the one place the plan pushes against a principle, and the trade is deliberate.
   "Principles are stated at altitude" says anything enumerable is first a materialized view,
   regenerated and `--verify`-gated, with `supported-directives.adoc` as the precedent. The
   alphabetical column *is* enumerable, and after step 3 the test derives exactly the set the
   column restates. What the principle is actually guarding against is the unguarded census, and
   the guard closes that: a hand list whose every entry a build-time test pins against the page set
   is no longer trusted prose. Generating the column outright stays available as a follow-up if the
   category grouping ever becomes derivable; it buys nothing extra while the grouping is editorial.

## Plan

1. **`docs/manual/reference/index.adoc`.** Replace the `== Directives` section (lines 28-90, the
   two-column table and both lists) with a short section in the established shape: one
   `xref:directives/index.adoc[...]` entry and a sentence describing what the directive reference
   covers. Keep the section's position in the page. No inbound anchor links into that section
   exist, so nothing breaks (verified: no `reference/index.adoc#...` xrefs anywhere in the tree).

2. **`docs/manual/reference/directives/index.adoc`.** The alphabetical column is complete and stays
   as-is. The *By category* column needs two additions before the step-3 guard can go green: add
   `xref:referenceFor.adoc[@referenceFor]` to *Joining* beside `@reference` (it is the
   reference-selection companion, and no other category fits), and restore
   `xref:record.adoc[@record]` to *Mapping* with the same `(deprecated, ignored)` annotation the
   stale copy carries. This page then becomes the single hand-maintained list.

3. **`DirectiveDocCoverageTest`.** Add a second test method asserting index completeness against
   the page set already computed by `pagesFromDocs()`:
   - the set of directive pages xref'd from the *alphabetical* column of
     `directives/index.adoc` equals the page set exactly (catches both a page with no entry and
     an entry pointing at a deleted page);
   - every page is named at least once somewhere in the *By category* column (catches a
     directive added to the alphabetical list but never filed under a category).

   Two mechanics the implementer should not have to guess:

   - *Splitting the columns.* Both lists live inside one `[cols="1,1"]` / `|===` block, so the only
     structural delimiter is the `== By category` heading on line 43 (the cell break `a|` is not a
     reliable anchor). Split the file text on that heading and match xrefs within each half. Do not
     regex the whole file for the category check: the two columns overlap heavily, so a whole-file
     match makes that assertion tautological and the guard silently vacuous.
   - *Names versus filenames.* `pagesFromDocs()` returns *directive names*, having already applied
     `PAGE_TO_DIRECTIVE` (`index-directive` becomes `index`). Index xrefs are *page filenames*. Map
     one side to the other explicitly (an inverse of `PAGE_TO_DIRECTIVE`, or a filename-level
     sibling of `pagesFromDocs()`) rather than comparing the two vocabularies directly. Keep
     `NON_DIRECTIVE_PAGES` filtering the landing page itself out of the compared set.

   Extend the class javadoc: the seam is now SDL to pages to index, not just SDL to pages.

4. **Sanity sweep.** Grep `docs/` for any other page reproducing a multi-directive roll-up list
   that would become a third copy. Fold anything found into step 1 or note it for a follow-up
   item; do not expand the guard to police arbitrary pages.

## Acceptance criteria

- `docs/manual/reference/index.adoc` carries no per-directive bullet list; its Directives section
  is a single pointer to `reference/directives/index.adoc`, structurally consistent with the
  Runtime API, Maven plugin, Editor support, Diagnostics, and Deprecations sections on the same
  page.
- Navigating manual → Reference → Directives reaches a list containing `@pivot`, `@asFacet`,
  `@routine`, `@scalarType`, and `@tenantFanOut`.
- `DirectiveDocCoverageTest` fails when a page under `reference/directives/` has no alphabetical
  entry in `directives/index.adoc`, and fails when it has no category mention. Demonstrate both
  by temporarily removing an entry, not by inspection.
- `DirectiveDocCoverageTest` fails when `directives/index.adoc` xrefs a page that does not exist.
- The *By category* column of `directives/index.adoc` names all 29 directive pages, `@referenceFor`
  and `@record` included.
- `mvn install -Plocal-db` green, including the AsciiDoctor render and the Javadoc reference
  gate.

## Out of scope

- Content review of the five previously-unlinked pages. They exist and read as complete; auditing
  their accuracy against the emitters is separate work.
- The two withheld-from-v1 directives (`@experimental_constructType`, `@sourceRow`). Their
  page-less state is deliberate and already covered by the existing carve-out.
- Generating any part of the directive index from the SDL (option 3 above).
